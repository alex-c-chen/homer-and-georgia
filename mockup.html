"In 1-3 sentences, explain why Germany's alliance with Austria-Hungary created a diplomatic risk by 1914…"
✓ Correct — good grasp of the key tension
After Bismarck's dismissal, Wilhelm II pursued more aggressive foreign policy. The alliance meant that Austria's dispute with Serbia automatically dragged Germany into a wider conflict
Good answer. You've nailed the core tension. One thing to sharpen: Bismarck's original design was the Dual Alliance (1879) as a defensive pact — it only obligated Germany if Austria was attacked, not if Austria was the aggressor.

By 1914, Austria's ultimatum to Serbia was offensive in nature, yet Germany issued its "blank cheque." That's the drift you're describing — but it's worth naming the blank cheque explicitly in an exam answer.
What was the blank cheque exactly?
On July 5–6, 1914, Kaiser Wilhelm II and Chancellor Bethmann Hollweg privately assured Austria-Hungary of Germany's "unconditional support" for whatever action Austria chose to take against Serbia — essentially a blank cheque to escalate.
